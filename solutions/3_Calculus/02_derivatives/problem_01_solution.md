### Problem 1. Derivative of a Polynomial and the Slope of a Tangent Line
For

$$
f(x)=3x^5-2x^3+7x-4
$$

find $f'(x)$. Then compute $f'(0)$ and $f'(1)$, determine the signs of the obtained values, and explain what they say about the slope of the tangent line to the graph at these two points.

> **Why this problem:** it reinforces the power rule and connects the value of the derivative at a point with the geometric meaning of the tangent slope.

## Solution

Apply the power rule $(x^m)'=mx^{m-1}$ term by term:

$$
f'(x)=15x^4-6x^2+7.
$$

Thus $f'(0)=7>0$ and $f'(1)=15-6+7=16>0$. Both tangent lines rise from left to right; the tangent at $x=1$ is steeper.

For verification of the geometric interpretation, $f(0)=-4$ and $f(1)=4$, so the tangent lines are $y=7x-4$ and $y-4=16(x-1)$, respectively. Each passes through its stated point and has the computed derivative as slope.
