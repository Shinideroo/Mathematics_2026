### Problem 10. The Tangent Line as an Application of the Derivative
For

$$
f(x)=x^2-3x+1
$$

find the equation of the tangent line at the point with $x=2$. Give the point of tangency, the value $f'(2)$, the equation of the line, and verify that the point lies on the obtained tangent line.

> **Why this problem:** it combines the derivative and the equation of a line into a complete geometric problem.

## Solution

The derivative is $f'(x)=2x-3$. At $x=2$,

$$
f(2)=4-6+1=-1,\qquad f'(2)=1.
$$

Using point-slope form, the tangent line is

$$
y-(-1)=1(x-2),\qquad y=x-3.
$$

The point of tangency is $(2,-1)$, and substituting $x=2$ in the line gives $y=-1$.

For an additional tangency check, $f(x)-(x-3)=x^2-4x+4=(x-2)^2$. The difference vanishes to second order at $2$, consistent with equal values and slopes there.
