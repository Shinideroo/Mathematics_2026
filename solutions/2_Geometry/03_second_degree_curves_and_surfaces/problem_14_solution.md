### Exercise 14. Cone and Cylinder
Compare

$$
x^2+y^2=z^2
$$

and

$$
x^2+y^2=4.
$$

For each surface, describe the cross-sections $z=c$, $x=0$, and $y=0$, make a sketch, and explain why the radius of the cross-section depends on $z$ in the first case but is constant in the second.

> **Why this exercise:** teaches how to distinguish a cone from a cylinder by the behavior of their cross-sections.

## Solution

**First surface, $x^2+y^2=z^2$:**

- $z=c$: circle of radius $|c|$, collapsing to a point for $c=0$.
- $x=0$: $y^2=z^2$, the two lines $y=\pm z$.
- $y=0$: $x^2=z^2$, the two lines $x=\pm z$.

It is a double circular cone with vertex at the origin.

**Second surface, $x^2+y^2=4$:**

- $z=c$: circle of radius $2$ for every $c$.
- $x=0$: lines $y=\pm2$, with $z$ arbitrary.
- $y=0$: lines $x=\pm2$, with $z$ arbitrary.

It is a circular cylinder of radius $2$ around $Oz$.

Schematic axial profiles (rotate around the vertical axis):

~~~text
Cone                 Cylinder
\       /            |       |
 \     /             |       |
  \   /              |       |
   \ /               |       |
    O                |       |
   / \               |       |
  /   \              |       |
~~~

The first equation ties the squared radius to $z^2$, so the radius changes with height. The second contains no $z$, so translation along $Oz$ leaves its radius unchanged. Points $(|c|,0,c)$ and $(2,0,c)$ verify the respective section radii.
