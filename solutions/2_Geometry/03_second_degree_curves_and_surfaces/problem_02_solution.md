### Exercise 2. Circle by Completing the Square
Reduce

$$
x^2+y^2-6x+4y-3=0
$$

to canonical form. Find the center and radius, then substitute two simple points from the circle into both forms of the equation.

> **Why this exercise:** practices completing the square as a transition from a general equation to the geometry of the object.

## Solution

Complete each square:

$$
x^2-6x=(x-3)^2-9,\qquad y^2+4y=(y+2)^2-4.
$$

Therefore the equation becomes

$$
(x-3)^2+(y+2)^2=16.
$$

The center is $(3,-2)$ and radius is $4$.

Choose $(7,-2)$ and $(-1,-2)$. In canonical form, each gives $16+0=16$. In the original form the respective checks are

$$
49+4-42-8-3=0,\qquad
1+4+6-8-3=0.
$$

Completing the square identifies the translation of a standard circle without changing its point set.
