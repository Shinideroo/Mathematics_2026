### Exercise 6. Shifted Ellipse
Reduce

$$
4x^2+9y^2-16x+18y-11=0
$$

to canonical form. Give the center and semiaxis lengths. Check that substituting the coordinates of the center into the left-hand side gives a value consistent with the form you obtained.

> **Why this exercise:** reinforces completing the square and recognizing a shift of the coordinate system.

## Solution

Group terms and complete squares:

$$
4[(x-2)^2-4]+9[(y+1)^2-1]-11=0.
$$

Hence

$$
4(x-2)^2+9(y+1)^2=36,
\qquad
\frac{(x-2)^2}{9}+\frac{(y+1)^2}{4}=1.
$$

This is an ellipse centered at $(2,-1)$, with horizontal semiaxis $3$ and vertical semiaxis $2$.

At the center, the original left-hand side is $16+9-32-18-11=-36$. The completed-square expression is also $0+0-36=-36$. The center lies inside the ellipse, not on its boundary, exactly as the canonical form predicts.
