### Exercise 7. Classifying a Second-Degree Equation
Determine the type of object described by:

1. $4x^2+9y^2=36$,
2. $x^2-y^2=1$,
3. $x^2-4y=0$,
4. $x^2+y^2+1=0$.

In the last case, explain why there are no real points.

> **Why this exercise:** develops quick recognition of the curve type and checking whether real solutions exist.

## Solution

Reduce each equation to a familiar form:

1. $4x^2+9y^2=36$ becomes $x^2/9+y^2/4=1$: an ellipse centered at the origin, with semiaxes $3$ and $2$.
2. $x^2-y^2=1$ is a hyperbola centered at the origin, with vertices $(\pm1,0)$ and asymptotes $y=\pm x$.
3. $x^2-4y=0$ becomes $y=x^2/4$: a parabola with vertex $(0,0)$, vertical symmetry axis, opening upward.
4. $x^2+y^2+1=0$ has **no real points**, since its left side is at least $1$.

The signs and number of squared coordinates distinguish the types. As quick checks, $(3,0)$ lies on the ellipse, $(1,0)$ on the hyperbola, and $(2,1)$ on the parabola. The inequality in case 4 proves emptiness without trying individual points.
