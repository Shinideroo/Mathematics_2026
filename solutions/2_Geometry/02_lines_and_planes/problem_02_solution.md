### Exercise 2. A Line in Space
Given

$$
L:(x,y,z)=(2,-1,3)+t(1,2,-2).
$$

State a point on the line and its direction vector, check whether $P=(4,3,-1)$ lies on $L$, and find the point corresponding to $t=-1$.

> **Why this exercise:** reinforces the interpretation of parametric form as “point + direction.”

## Solution

The point-plus-direction form immediately identifies the point $(2,-1,3)$ and direction vector $(1,2,-2)$.

For $P=(4,3,-1)$, the first coordinate gives $2+t=4$, so $t=2$. Check the other coordinates: $-1+2(2)=3$ and $3-2(2)=-1$. Hence $P$ lies on $L$.

For $t=-1$,

$$
(x,y,z)=(2,-1,3)-(1,2,-2)=(1,-3,5).
$$

All coordinates use the same parameter; this is essential for checking line membership.
